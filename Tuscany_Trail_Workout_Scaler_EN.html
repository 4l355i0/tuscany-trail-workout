Purpose

The goal is not to reproduce 4–5 hours of riding on an indoor trainer. The goal is to create a compact, approximately 45-minute workout that preserves the meaningful power demands of each Tuscany Trail day: climbs, surges, peak efforts, and the order in which those efforts occur.

Long descents and very low-power sections are deliberately compressed because they add little training value indoors.

1. From GPX to gradient

Each GPX track provides latitude, longitude and elevation. Distance is calculated along the track, then elevation is resampled every 100 m and smoothed to reduce GPS altitude noise.

The local gradient is calculated over approximately 500 m:

Gradient [%] = 100 × Δh / Δs

Example: 30 m of elevation gain over 500 m gives a 6% average gradient.

2. Reference speed by gradient

A conservative bikepacking speed is assigned to each gradient range. This speed is not derived from FTP; it is an assumption used to estimate the physical power demand of the route.

3. Physical power model

Required power is estimated from gravitational resistance, rolling resistance and aerodynamic drag:

P = v / η × [ m g sin(θ) + m g Crr cos(θ) + ½ ρ CdA v² ]

Where:

ParameterReference value
Total mass, m90 kg
Gravity, g9.81 m/s²
Rolling resistance, Crr0.008
Aerodynamic area, CdA0.40 m²
Air density, ρ1.225 kg/m³
Drivetrain efficiency, η0.97

The three force terms are:

Fgravity = m g sin(θ)
Frolling = m g Crr cos(θ)
Faero = ½ ρ CdA v²

FTP does not enter this physical calculation. The route first generates a power requirement. FTP is only used later to interpret how difficult that power is for a given rider.

4. Smoothing and macro-blocks

Power is smoothed across neighbouring 500 m segments to remove unrealistic fluctuations caused by GPS noise. Consecutive segments with similar power are then merged into macro-blocks.

This avoids creating trainer steps such as 121 → 126 → 119 → 124 W when they represent one continuous effort.

A 75% workout therefore means 75% of the estimated route power, not 75% of the rider's FTP.

6. Time compression

The original days are roughly 4–5 hours long. Indoor versions are compressed to about 45 minutes. The purpose is to preserve the relative training importance of the meaningful route sections rather than simply applying the same time reduction to every segment.

Training Stress Score is calculated as:

TSS = hours × IF² × 100
IF = NP / FTP

Macro-blocks are weighted according to their relative training load. Very easy sections are compressed much more aggressively than climbing or high-power sections.

7. Low-power sections

Long descents or sections requiring only a few tens of watts would be pointless to reproduce literally on a smart trainer. Therefore low-power route sections are consolidated, shortened, and represented at approximately 85 W in the reference workout.

This intentionally reduces global variability compared with the road profile, but preserves the parts that matter most for training: meaningful efforts, peaks, and their sequence.

8. Smart-trainer rationalisation

Very short power changes are not useful in ERG mode. The final files therefore avoid micro-intervals and generally use blocks of at least about one minute.

Adjacent steps are merged only when the change has minimal effect on the workout's key metrics.

9. Validation

The rationalised smart-trainer versions were checked against the higher-resolution 75% workouts using:

  • Normalised Power (NP)
  • Variability Index (VI = NP / average power)
  • Intensity Factor (IF)
  • TSS
  • Total work in kJ
  • Peak power

10. Important limitations

This is a training model, not a route simulator. Actual outdoor power demand will also depend on surface condition, wind, tyre pressure, rider position, stops, technical terrain, fatigue, temperature and pacing decisions.

The 45-minute workouts do not reproduce the musculoskeletal and nutritional demands of riding for 4–5 hours. Long outdoor rides remain necessary for full event preparation.
